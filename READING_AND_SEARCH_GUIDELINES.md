# Reading and Search Guidelines

This document captures the retrieval and reading rules that should shape future data work in QueryPat. It is about how the corpus should be searched, how documents should be read, and what the summaries and indexes need to preserve for those tasks to work well.

## 1. Search is evidence retrieval

Search should not act like a plain keyword dump. It should help a reader move from a query to the right evidence bucket.

Priority search facets:
- evidentiary lane
- document type
- date or date range
- people mentioned
- works discussed
- places and institutional sites
- themes and dictionary terms
- contradiction zones

Keyword matching is useful, but it is only one input. The higher-value result is the document that answers the research question with the fewest hops.

## 2. Read from structure outward

When reading a document, start with the structured metadata before the prose body.

Recommended reading order:
1. title, author, date, and lane
2. card summary
3. page summary
4. people, works, places, and terms
5. contradiction notes and reliability flags
6. full text or excerpted passages

This keeps the reader from mistaking a long document for a strong one, or a short summary for a weak one.

## 3. Preserve distinctions

The archive is contested. Reading rules should preserve the difference between:
- PKD's self-report
- biography
- scholarship
- fiction
- primary documents by other people

If a summary or index entry blurs those categories, it should be rewritten.

## 4. What summaries must preserve

Document summaries should preserve the search keys a researcher will need later.

At minimum, a useful summary should tell the reader:
- what the document is
- why it matters
- what it says or argues
- who and what it discusses
- what locations, universities, archives, or conferences it names
- what it corroborates or disputes

For biography, interview, and archive documents, locatable places are first-class metadata, not incidental prose.

## 5. Reading rules by content type

### Biography and memoir
- Surface the biographer's frame.
- Name the original material.
- Surface the contradictions.
- Promote universities, lecture sites, and archives into the map and timeline when they are identifiable.

### Interviews
- Keep the interview date distinct from publication date.
- Treat claims as self-report.
- Surface events, places, and institutions that can be added to the biography timeline.

### Scholarship
- State the argument before the bibliography.
- Keep the scholar's interpretive position visible.
- Preserve named disagreements and the works they answer.

### Primary documents
- Tag the lane clearly.
- Preserve venue, occasion, and audience.
- Keep the excerpt length fair and the self-report status obvious.

### Places and people records
- Distinguish acquaintance from later interpretation.
- Distinguish character inspiration from personal relationship.
- Keep location summaries short, but explicit about relevance.

## 6. Searchable categories should stay aligned with the ontology

The ontology and the search index should use the same conceptual buckets whenever possible:
- biography events
- works
- people
- places
- themes
- dictionary terms
- documents
- universities and institutional sites

If a new entity type is added, the search and reading rules should be updated at the same time.

## 7. Practical rule for future updates

Before adding or rewriting a document summary, ask:
- Would a researcher find this by searching for a person, a work, a place, a university, or a theme?
- Does the summary contain the terms that a future search would need?
- Does the summary preserve the lane and the contradiction status?

If the answer is no, the summary is not ready yet.
