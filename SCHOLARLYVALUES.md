# Scholarly Values for QueryPat

QueryPat is a digital humanities project about Philip K. Dick that should make
evidence easier to find, compare, and argue with. It should not flatten PKD into
a single explanation. The project is strongest when it lets primary documents,
biographical synthesis, fiction, the Exegesis, and later scholarship sit in
productive tension.

## Evidence Before Gloss

Every interpretive claim should be traceable to a source. Prefer primary sources
when describing what PKD said, wrote, reported, feared, believed, or revised.
Use secondary sources to frame, challenge, and contextualize those reports, not
to erase the difference between evidence and interpretation.

When an event comes from a letter, preserve the letter date, recipient, volume,
and letter id. When an event comes from a biography or scholarly source, preserve
the source's stance and caution level. A skeptical psychobiographical reading and
a religious self-report can coexist if their lanes are clear.

## Primary-Source Density

The biography should become denser, especially around periods where the usual
outline of PKD's life is too thin: adolescence, record-store work, early reading,
marriages and relationships, drug use, medical crises, composition periods,
unpublished projects, and religious experience.

The Selected Letters are not merely support material. They are a primary
chronological backbone for:

- what PKD was working on and when
- who he was writing to and intellectually testing ideas with
- how he narrated his own life in real time
- how his explanations of 2-3-74 changed by audience
- his relationships with SF writers, editors, spouses, scholars, fans, and critics

## Named Correspondence Matters

Letters to major SF authors and intellectual figures deserve special handling as
documents, not just as extracted events. Correspondence with Ursula K. Le Guin,
Roger Zelazny, John Brunner, Robert Heinlein, Harlan Ellison, Julian Jaynes,
Patricia Warrick, Claudia Bush, Paul Williams, and others should be searchable by
person, date, theme, and work.

Corpus-level wrappers are useful when a relationship or exchange is bigger than
one letter. The Claudia Bush letters are a good model: they should function both
as individual primary sources and as a coherent interpretive correspondence.

## Quote Sparingly, But Quote

Short quotations matter because PKD's phrasing often carries the interpretive
charge: a phrase can reveal uncertainty, intensity, humor, paranoia, religious
commitment, or self-mythologizing better than paraphrase alone.

Use quotations to anchor claims, especially when PKD:

- defines a visionary experience
- compares fiction to life
- names a drug, illness, or treatment
- comments on another author
- revises an earlier account
- gives a date, location, relationship status, or composition detail

Keep quotations brief and purposeful. The site's job is not to reproduce the
library; it is to point readers to the right evidence with enough texture to know
why it matters.

## Contradiction Is Data

Do not hide contradictions. PKD often retold the same event differently across
letters, interviews, fiction, and the Exegesis. Biographers also disagree. Those
differences should be modeled as useful evidence.

Important contradiction zones include:

- 2-3-74 and its causes
- drug use versus religious experience
- the 1971 break-in
- the role of amphetamines, LSD, sodium pentothal, and vitamin regimes
- the Christopher diagnosis
- the identity of the AI Voice, Zebra, Sophia, VALIS, and Thomas
- the influence of Bishop Pike, Jaynes, Bogen, Ornstein, and Jung
- relationships with Anne, Nancy, Tessa, Linda Levy, Doris Sauter, Joan Simpson,
  and other women in Dick's life

## Religious Experience Deserves Double Vision

The project should take PKD's religious claims seriously without being credulous.
It should also take psychiatric, neurological, and drug-related explanations
seriously without reducing the religious material to pathology.

Kyle Arnold's The Divine Madness of Philip K. Dick is especially valuable here
because it gives a sustained psychobiographical account of visionary experience,
trauma, paranoia, drug use, and religious interpretation. It should be used as a
major interpretive source, especially for 2-3-74, the pink light, the AI Voice,
X-Kalay, amphetamine-related paranoia, and the afterlife of these experiences in
the Exegesis and late novels.

## Composition Chronology Is Biography

Writing periods are life events. The project should track when PKD was drafting,
revising, abandoning, selling, or reinterpreting each story and novel. This is
especially important when a work is tied to a relationship, illness, financial
pressure, drug regime, move, religious reading, or correspondence.

The biography should connect life events to works without collapsing fiction
into diary. Fiction is evidence, but it is not transparent evidence.

## Unpublished And Pending Sources Count

Known but unlocated documents should be represented as pending objects rather
than forgotten. A pending document record makes the research need explicit:
source needed, OCR needed, summary needed, claims not yet extracted.

This applies to items such as Maze of Death Theology and other unpublished or
semi-archival documents. Once a source file is supplied, it should move from
metadata-only to full ingestion, with a document summary, text extraction,
claim/event mining, and cross-links.

## Build For Re-Search

Every extraction pass should leave behind a reproducible method: scripts,
reports, source ids, hit lists, and skip notes. The goal is not just to add facts
but to improve the machine for finding better facts later.

Useful reports include:

- source-search audits over letters and biographies
- gap reports by year, person, work, and event type
- contradiction registries
- document wrappers for important correspondence clusters
- extraction queues with explicit next actions

## Humane Interface, Scholarly Backbone

The website should be browsable and inviting, but the underlying data should
remain rigorous. A reader should be able to move from a clean biography summary
to a primary document, from a document to related terms and works, and from a
term to the evidence behind it.

The ideal QueryPat reader is not only consuming a narrative. They are learning
how to investigate PKD.
