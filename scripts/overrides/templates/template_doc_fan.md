# Template — Fan Publications

For: fanzines, fan-press monographs, convention publications, fan-edited critical volumes.
Lane: **C** (Scholarship) for fan-academic hybrids that contribute to the scholarly literature; **D** (Synthesis) for editorial / curatorial fan publications; **E** (Primary) where they preserve PKD's letters or contemporary fan responses.

## card_summary
**Length:** 60–90 words.

**Required structure:**
1. Publication name, editor, era of publication, frequency, format.
2. Distinguishing role — what makes this fanzine matter to PKD studies.
3. Era covered or specific issue, if a single-issue summary.

## page_summary
**Length:** 200–500 words.

**Required sections:**

1. **Publication identity** — name, editor(s), publisher (often self-published), period of operation, format (xeroxed zine / offset / digital), distribution.
2. **Editorial program** — what the publication covers and how. Some fan publications are scholarship-adjacent (Bruce Gillespie's *SF Commentary*, Frank Hollander's *PKD Otaku*); some are convention-focused (*Niekas*); some are reader-discussion (*PKDS Newsletter*). Name the orientation.
3. **Notable contents** — significant articles, contributors, recovered correspondence, fan-research debuts. Where the fanzine published something that academic scholarship later cites, name it explicitly.
4. **Contributors** — the fan-critics and academics who wrote here. Many academic careers have fanzine roots (Robinson contributed to fanzines before *The Novels*); scholarly conversations have happened in fanzines that then migrated to journals.
5. **Position in PKD studies** — how academic scholars cite or rely on this publication. Some fanzines hold materials not duplicated in any academic source.
6. **Fan reception layer** — what readers wrote, debated, contested. Fan publications preserve a strand of PKD reception unavailable from academic sources.

## Required JSON fields
- `category` — `fanzines` or `fan_publications`
- `evidentiary_lane` — `C`, `D`, or `E` per above
- `source_reliability` — `fan_press` or `fan_scholarship`

## Editorial principle
Fan publications are part of PKD studies, not below it. The "all key contributions both academic and fan" rule from CONTENT_PLAN_V3.md §1 applies here especially: when a fanzine hosts a debate, recovers a letter, or publishes a memoir, the contribution must be named.

## Lint checklist
- [ ] Names the editor(s).
- [ ] Names the editorial program.
- [ ] Names notable contents specific to PKD studies.
- [ ] Distinguishes fan-academic hybrids from purely fan-discussion venues.
- [ ] Cross-links to scholars who contributed.
