As to advice for beginning writers ... the most important thing (I’m sure you’ve been told this many times, but it’s still true) is: write about what you actually know. Don’t ignore the possibilities of drama in your life and the lives of those around you; there’s drama in the most “ordinary” human life. Second, read the great prose writ­ ers: Faulkner, Hemingway, Hunter S. Thompson, William Burroughs, Saul Bellow, Herbert Gold. You can’t write if you haven’t read. Third, keep a journal; note down your observations, what you see, what you hear people say, your reaction to events, your thoughts. Fourth, don’t pay too much attention to negative criticism. Everyone fancies himself a critic. Go on your own instincts; write something, put it away for a week, read it over, see how _you_ like it. And hang in there; writing is a lot of fun but also it’s a lot of work. And good luck; you’ll need it.
