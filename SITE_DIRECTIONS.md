# Site Directions

This file captures the live editorial directions that should keep steering the PKD site as it grows. It exists so the map, timeline, people records, and archive descriptions keep following the same rules across future sessions.

## 1. Site identity

- The user-facing site is the **Philip K. Dick Knowledge Portal**.
- `QueryPat` remains the repository codename.
- The *Exegesis* is one important source, but not the whole project.
- The dashboard should read as PKD-as-author first, with the *Exegesis* as a discrete part of the site.

## 2. Map and geography

- The map should be California-first for now, but the catalog should collect all relevant places so expansion stays evidence-based.
- Color-code locations by category.
- Core map categories should include:
  - biography
  - novel setting
  - short story setting
  - event
  - institutional site
  - archive / special collection
- Keep hover tooltips and click popups on markers.
- Every marker should include a short relevance summary, not just a place name.
- Include places where PKD lived, worked, studied, lectured, visited, or was buried.
- Include universities, archives, conferences, special collections, and other institutional PKD sites.
- Include biographical routes and travel traces when they are historically useful.
- If a place is only approximate, say so honestly.

## 3. Timeline

- The timeline should not be Exegesis-only.
- Include publications, letters, interviews, university appearances, lectures, conferences, residences, and other biography-linked events.
- University experiences mentioned in interviews belong on the biography timeline as well as in the map when location data exists.

## 4. People

- The `people who knew PKD` section should include more than direct acquaintances.
- It should also include:
  - people who matter in the Exegesis and unpublished writings
  - major correspondents and interlocutors
  - people who inspired or were transformed into novel characters
  - people central to interview testimony or later scholarship
- Examples that should be treated as significant, not thin stubs:
  - Claudia Bush
  - K.W. Jeter
  - Tim Powers
  - Bishop Pike
  - Alan Watts
- Distinguish direct personal contact from character inspiration or second-order scholarly importance.

## 5. Evidence discipline

- Keep lane distinctions visible.
- Do not collapse fiction, self-report, scholarship, and synthesis into one undifferentiated story.
- Surface contradictions rather than smoothing them over.
- Summaries should explain why a place or person matters, not just label it.

## 6. Practical rule

- If a new item is about PKD's life, work, friendships, universities, archive history, or fiction geography, assume it belongs in at least one of:
  - the map
  - the timeline
  - the people index
  - the archive catalog
- Prefer adding evidence-backed structure over leaving the item as a loose note.
